## Problem Statement

`app/streamlit_app.py` renders nine charts in one flat vertical scroll. The sidebar holds
one input widget — the date-range picker at line 49 — under an `st.header("Filters")` at
line 46, with an `st.caption` naming the data source at line 58. There is no section
navigation, no visible structure, and no indication that the nine charts are not nine equal
things.

The user watched someone use the app and **they stopped scrolling before reaching the later
charts**. The request that came out of that: a left navigation menu, and smoother
interaction.

The complication, and the reason this document is longer than the request:

| # | Chart | Role | Lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crashes by borough, unlabeled shown | **the claim** | 111-132 |
| 2 | Why those rows have no borough | **the mechanism** | 135-146 |
| 3 | Indexed crashes / injuries / deaths | **the implication** | 151-173 |
| 4-9 | Monthly, factors, victims, day×hour, geo, severity | the nine-chart requirement | 175-255 |

Charts 1-3 carry the entire argument. Charts 4-9 exist because `PROJECT_GUIDE.md` specified
nine charts. The prior design doc is explicit that this project must not be a chart gallery
("a nine-chart dashboard on a public dataset makes no claim, so there is nothing for anyone
to ask about"), and the current page satisfies that only by ordering — the argument happens
to come first.

**A conventional nav makes this worse.** Nine equally-weighted links in a sidebar is the
gallery problem relocated, and the most clickable item in that list is "8. Where crashes
cluster," because a map beats a bar chart every time. The nav has to encode the hierarchy
or it actively undoes the one structural decision the page currently gets right.

## Demand Evidence

**Strongest evidence: one live observation.** The user watched a real person use the app
and that person stopped scrolling before reaching the later charts. Not a survey, not a
guess — behavior, observed.

Its limits, stated plainly:

- **n = 1.** One person, once.
- **The stopping chart was not recorded.** This is the load-bearing gap. If they stopped
  after chart 4, they consumed the entire argument and correctly declined the appendix,
  which means the page worked and there is no defect. If they stopped at chart 2, the
  argument never landed and none of the work below matters. The same observation supports
  opposite conclusions and nobody knows which.
- **It happened on localhost**, over a shoulder or a screen share, because the app is not
  deployed. There is currently no mechanism to produce a second observation.

Everything downstream inherits that uncertainty. The design below is deliberately built so
that it is correct under **either** reading: if the observer quit early because the appendix
was in the way, demoting charts 4-9 fixes it; if they quit early having already got the
argument, the claim-labelled rail means they left with the thesis anyway.

**Prior demand evidence for the project as a whole** (carried from the prior design doc,
still the only real signal in the portfolio): someone asked, unprompted, about the Customer
Support Triage Console — the one project that argues something. Nobody asked about the two
capstone PDFs.

## Status Quo

**What a visitor lives with today.** They land on `streamlit_app.py:66` and before the first
pixel of any chart they must get through:

1. An `st.title` — the headline claim
2. Two dense `st.caption` paragraphs (lines 71-83), one reconciling 39.8% against 44.2%
3. A conditional red `st.error` banner if the app is on the dev fixture (lines 85-91)
4. Four `st.metric` tiles (lines 100-106)
5. An `st.divider`

That is a wall of prose and numbers standing between the visitor and the visual payoff.
Both captions are there for good reason — they were added by `/qa` on 2026-08-09 to fix
ISSUE-001 and ISSUE-002, where the header contradicted its own KPI. They are load-bearing
and must not be deleted.

**How the reader currently locates anything:** scrolling. Section headings are numbered
`st.subheader` calls with Streamlit's auto-generated anchors, derived from heading text and
therefore silently changed by any copy edit.

**What the app does not do today:** it is not deployed. There is no URL. Roadmap rows
"Borough recovery + agreement check" and "Live on Streamlit Community Cloud" are both
`not started`.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user** (unchanged from the prior design doc, re-confirmed this session): the person
who already has the interview and clicks the link during or after it. Not the resume
screener, who statistically will not click. They arrive knowing nothing about the borough
gap, they give it well under two minutes, and the outcome that matters is whether they ask a
question about the finding.

**Narrowest wedge, chosen this session:** a left rail with section names and a sense of
where you are. The reading-progress half was cut in review — see Premise 2.

**Wedge as refined:** a left rail whose three top items are the three claims in sentence
form, above a collapsed group holding the other six. The refinement changes what happens in
the failure case. With chart-name links, a reader who quits early leaves with nothing. With
claim links, the rail alone states the thesis, so quitting early costs them nothing.

**The rail labels carry no numbers.** This is a hard constraint, not a style preference. A
static figure in the sidebar sits directly above a date filter that changes it, which is
ISSUE-001 — "a visitor sees two numbers for one claim and trusts neither" — rebuilt in the
sidebar. `streamlit_app.py:114` already renders a live per-range figure in bold, and the
caption at lines 79-83 exists specifically to warn the reader those two numbers differ.
Any numeric rail label would reintroduce the bug the captions were written to fix.

Concretely, the three labels are claims about the dataset's **structure**, which stay true
under every filter:

```
THE ARGUMENT
  Crashes with no borough get dropped
  The dropped ones are on highways
  Crashes and deaths don't move together

SUPPORTING DETAIL  ▸
```

Three sentences. Read alone, with no scrolling, they are the thesis. Each must be supported
by its chart's own supporting text **in every branch that text can take** — which is a real
constraint, not a formality: chart 1's supporting sentence is an `st.markdown` at lines
113-116 (its `st.caption` at 130-132 is about fatality rate, a different claim), chart 2's
caption at 145-146 renders only in the `else` branch and is replaced by a bare `st.info` when
`streets` is empty, and chart 3's caption at 169-171 renders only when `len(yr) > 1`. On a
one-month filter the rail would otherwise assert "The dropped ones are on highways" directly
above an empty-state placeholder.

## Constraints

**Framework.**
- Streamlit reruns the entire script on any widget interaction. Scroll position is not
  preserved across a rerun. This rules out every scroll-linked or state-driven reveal.
- CSS injected via `unsafe_allow_html` is re-injected on every rerun and applied with a
  delay, which is a documented community complaint. It is the ceiling on how smooth any
  hand-rolled animation can be, and it is why the motion budget here is scroll behavior
  plus `:hover` / `:focus` transitions — both browser-native and unaffected by rerun timing.
- **`st.plotly_chart` will not honour `fig.update_layout(transition=...)` in any useful way
  here.** Treat this as "assume no benefit, not worth verifying" rather than a proven
  mechanism. Either way it is out of scope.
- Streamlit auto-collapses the sidebar below roughly 768px. On a phone the rail is behind a
  hamburger and delivers none of its value. See Open Question 2.
- **`st.expander` executes its body unconditionally.** Putting charts 4-9 in an expander
  buys demotion only — all six queries still run and all six figures, including the Plotly
  map, are still built on every rerun. There is no load-time or memory win on the ~1 GB
  container. Gating that would need `session_state`, which costs a rerun. Out of scope.

**Dependencies.** `requirements.txt` carries an explicit header: runtime-only, keep it
minimal, ~1 GB of RAM, and no dependency that can fail in front of a visitor. Design
decisions 1 and 5 in the prior doc rest on the same principle. This feature adds **zero**
new packages.

The evaluated-and-rejected candidate: `streamlit-scroll-navigation` v1.2.2 (June 2026), a
React component whose `scroll_navbar()` does smooth anchor scroll with `auto_update_anchor`
scrollspy. Closest off-the-shelf match to the original request. Rejected because (a) its
PyPI classifiers claim Python 3.7-3.10 against this project's 3.12, (b) it is a React bundle
in a deploy whose architecture was chosen to have no runtime dependency that can break, and
(c) the only thing it adds over a plain `<a href="#anchor">` is scrollspy, which Premise 2
explicitly does not want.

**Repo state, verified this session rather than assumed:**
- `data/processed/crashes.parquet` is committed and tracked — 17.7 MB, whitelisted at
  `.gitignore:23-27` with a note recording that it supersedes design decision 16.
- `requirements.txt` is runtime-only and clean: streamlit, duckdb, pyarrow, pandas, plotly.
- No secret is needed at runtime. **Deploy is unblocked, roughly a 20-minute task.**
- `app/data.py:95-115` (`_ensure_recovery_columns`) already synthesises `borough_recovered`
  and `borough_source`, so the *schema* seam for the recovery exists.
- `sql/borough.sql` splits on `borough IS NULL`, not on `borough_source`. Chart 1 cannot
  show a reported/recovered toggle yet; `docs/QA-REPORT-2026-08-09.md:98-101` records that
  as untestable for exactly this reason. **Landing the toggle needs edits to `borough.sql`
  and to chart 1, plus the three-way slice non-negotiable 7 requires — it is not "a data
  change, not a code change."**
- `app/streamlit_app.py:20` uses a bare `from data import ...`, relying on Streamlit putting
  the entry script's directory on `sys.path`. It works locally. It is the single most likely
  thing to break the first Community Cloud deploy, so read the container log rather than
  assuming.
- **Charts 5 and 6 live inside an `st.columns(2)` block at lines 186-208**, with
  `st.divider()` at 183 and 210. Any move of charts 4-9 must carry that block and those
  dividers intact.

**Environment.** Python 3.12 as `py`. Streamlit is **not** importable from the base
interpreter and there is no `venv/` in the repo, so nothing in this document has been
verified against a running app. `py -m venv venv` and the install step come first; on this
network `uv` needs `--system-certs`.

## Premises

All five confirmed by the user this session. Premise 2 was revised mid-session after the
cross-model challenge; the revised form is what binds.

1. **The nav scrolls to in-page anchors. It does not split the app into pages.** All nine
   charts stay on one continuous document.
   *Reason, corrected during the session:* the original justification was that `st.Page`
   would let a visitor land on chart 5 without seeing the claim. That is wrong —
   `st.navigation` renders the default page first. The real reason is that splitting a
   continuous argument across page loads breaks the read.
2. **REVISED. The rail's job is to make the argument-vs-appendix hierarchy visible at a
   glance. It deliberately does not display remaining length.**
   *Original form:* "shows reading position and depth remaining — a commitment device, not a
   shortcut." Challenged on two grounds, both accepted: affordance beats intent (a list of
   clickable links *is* a jump menu regardless of the label), and telling a reader at chart 2
   that seven more await is a cost disclosure, which is the abandonment trigger rather than
   the antidote. **A "3 of 9" progress indicator is out of scope.** Note the honest tension:
   opening the Supporting-detail group reveals six items, which discloses the count. The rule
   is that depth is not disclosed *by default*, not that it is hidden from someone who asks.
3. **The rail encodes hierarchy.** Argument and supporting detail are visually unequal
   tiers. A flat list of nine equal items rebuilds the gallery problem in the sidebar.
4. **"Smooth animation" means scroll behavior plus CSS `:hover` / `:focus` transitions.**
   Not scroll-linked animation, not Plotly transitions, not scrollspy. The framework fights
   all three and wins. See "Honest note on 'more interactive'" below.
5. **Deploy and the borough recovery both outrank this feature.** The recovery is the finding
   the repo is named after and the README promises it; the deploy is what gives any of it an
   audience. Recommended order is stated in the Distribution Plan and this document is
   consistent with it.

## Approaches Considered

All three add zero dependencies. A and B touch only `app/streamlit_app.py`; C also adds
`app/sections.py` and `tests/test_sections.py` (see the sketch for why the spine cannot
live in the app module). They nest: A ⊂ B ⊂ C. **All three use explicit `anchor=` on every
heading**, so anchor drift on copy edits is prevented by A already; that is not a
differentiator.

Effort is quoted as **CC minutes / human hours** throughout, the human figure including
local venv setup and the empirical checks.

### Approach A: Chart-name rail
Explicit `anchor=` on each of the nine `st.subheader` calls. Sidebar renders a link list in
two groups — argument (3, always visible), supporting detail (6, inside a collapsed
`st.expander`) — plus the CSS block.
- **Pros:** ships in one sitting; cannot break the deploy; the two-tier split alone does
  most of the hierarchy work.
- **Cons:** nine chart names is still a menu of nine things; a reader who consults only the
  rail learns nothing about the finding; leaves charts 4-9 at full prominence in the page.
- **Reuses:** the nine existing `st.subheader` calls, the existing `with st.sidebar` block.
- **Effort:** ~25 lines changed. **CC ~20 min / human ~2 hrs** · **Risk:** Low ·
  **Completeness:** 7/10

### Approach B: Claim rail
Identical mechanics to A. The three argument links carry range-independent claim sentences
rather than chart names. **The sentences do not exist anywhere in the repo today and must be
written and checked against each chart's caption** — a small but real task A does not have.
- **Pros:** the only version where stopping early costs the reader nothing, because the rail
  alone states the thesis.
- **Cons:** claim sentences are worse for wayfinding if someone is hunting a specific chart;
  heavier sidebar; unconventional enough to read as odd rather than deliberate; the wording
  needs care to stay true under every date filter.
- **Reuses:** everything A reuses.
- **Effort:** ~25 lines + copy work. **CC ~25 min / human ~2.5 hrs** · **Risk:** Low ·
  **Completeness:** 8/10

### Approach C: Sectioned narrative — **SELECTED**
B, plus a module-level `SECTIONS` definition holding `(anchor, rail_label, heading, tier)`
for all nine, with the sidebar and the page headings rendered through it. Charts 4-9 move
into a demoted appendix block in the main column. CSS transitions on rail hover and focus.
- **Honest rationale**, corrected in review: `SECTIONS` does **not** buy drift protection —
  explicit `anchor=` already does that, in all three approaches. What it buys is one render
  loop instead of nine call sites, and the rail label sitting on the same line as the heading
  it describes, so the two are edited together rather than in two places 200 lines apart.
  That is a real but modest benefit, and the counterweight is a namedtuple registry in a
  260-line single-file script that a hiring manager will read.
- **Pros:** the appendix demotion is the change that most directly addresses the observed
  behavior; rail labels and headings cannot fall out of sync in a copy edit.
- **Cons:** largest first pass; the indirection reduces the file's readability; the appendix
  move is the riskiest element and is gated on Open Question 1.
- **Effort:** ~40-60 lines changed across three files. **CC ~45 min / human ~3-4 hrs** ·
  **Risk:** Low-Med · **Completeness:** 9/10

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C.** Selected by the user.

### Implementation sketch

**0. New module: `app/sections.py`.** The spine cannot live in `streamlit_app.py`. That
module imports streamlit at line 18 and then executes `st.set_page_config`,
`resolve_source()`, `get_connection()`, `date_bounds()` and possibly `st.stop()` at module
scope, so importing it from a test would run the entire app. `tests/test_date_range.py` only
works today because `data.py` is import-safe. Keep the spine import-safe the same way:

```python
"""app/sections.py — the page's structure, as pure data.

Deliberately imports nothing. streamlit_app.py renders from it and
tests/test_sections.py asserts over it; neither can happen if this module
pulls in streamlit.

One place where a section's key, anchor, sidebar label, and page heading are
written together, so a copy edit touches them at once instead of in two places
200 lines apart. Anchor STABILITY comes from passing anchor= explicitly at the
call site — not from this structure.

    key      what code refers to a section by; never rendered
    anchor   the HTML id. st.subheader(anchor=) writes it and the rail links to
             it. This string is written HERE and nowhere else.
    rail     what the SIDEBAR says. For argument rows this is the CLAIM, not the
             chart name, so a reader who consults only the rail leaves with the
             thesis. NEVER put a number here: the sidebar sits above a date
             filter that changes every figure, and a static number there is
             ISSUE-001 rebuilt. Each label must be supported by its chart's own
             supporting text, in every branch that text can take.
    heading  what the PAGE says above the chart
    tier     ARGUMENT (always visible) | DETAIL (behind the appendix)
"""
from collections import namedtuple

Section = namedtuple("Section", "key anchor rail heading tier")

ARGUMENT, DETAIL = "argument", "detail"

SECTIONS = (
    Section("borough", "borough-gap", "Crashes with no borough get dropped",
            "1. Crashes by borough, with the dropped rows shown", ARGUMENT),
    Section("missing", "why-missing", "The dropped ones are on highways",
            "2. Why those rows have no borough", ARGUMENT),
    Section("trend", "trend-divergence", "Crashes and deaths don't move together",
            "3. Crashes, injuries and deaths do not move together", ARGUMENT),
    Section("monthly",  "monthly",  "Monthly volume",        "4. Monthly volume", DETAIL),
    Section("factors",  "factors",  "Contributing factors",  "5. Contributing factors", DETAIL),
    Section("victims",  "victims",  "Who gets hurt",         "6. Who gets hurt", DETAIL),
    Section("when",     "when",     "When crashes happen",   "7. When crashes happen", DETAIL),
    Section("where",    "where",    "Where crashes cluster", "8. Where crashes cluster", DETAIL),
    Section("severity", "severity", "Severity outliers",     "9. Severity outliers", DETAIL),
)
SEC = {s.key: s for s in SECTIONS}
```

`SEC` is keyed by `key`, not by `anchor`, so a call site says `render_heading("borough")` and
the string `borough-gap` never appears anywhere but this file. The three argument sections
have keys that differ from their anchors precisely so this is checkable; the six detail rows
have them coincide, which is fine because those strings are still typed only here.

**1. Wire it in.** In `streamlit_app.py`, add `from sections import (ARGUMENT, DETAIL,
SECTIONS, SEC)` to the existing import block (lines 13-22), and define the two render
helpers immediately after the colour constants at line 30 — they must exist before the
post-guard call in step 3:

```python
def render_heading(key: str) -> None:
    s = SEC[key]
    st.subheader(s.heading, anchor=s.anchor)


def _links(tier: str) -> str:
    return "".join(f'<a href="#{s.anchor}">{s.rail}</a>'
                   for s in SECTIONS if s.tier == tier)


def render_rail() -> None:
    st.markdown("##### The argument")
    st.markdown(f'<div class="rail">{_links(ARGUMENT)}</div>', unsafe_allow_html=True)
    with st.expander("Supporting detail"):
        st.markdown(f'<div class="rail">{_links(DETAIL)}</div>', unsafe_allow_html=True)
```

Each existing `st.subheader("1. Crashes by borough...")` becomes `render_heading("borough")`.
The rail is emitted as one markdown block per tier rather than one per link, so Streamlit
does not wrap each link in its own `stMarkdown` element container with its own margins.
(Whether Streamlit's sanitiser preserves the `class` attribute at all is check 1b — the
single-block shape does not by itself guarantee it survives.)

The name is `render_heading`, not `heading`, so it does not collide with the `Section.heading`
field in a file the design says hiring managers will read.

**2. The CSS**, emitted immediately after `st.set_page_config` (line 25) and before
`st.title`. It re-runs on every rerun — that is Streamlit, not a bug to fix. Note that even
a `<style>`-only `st.markdown` produces an element container in the main pane, so keep it as
one call and check it adds no visible gap above the title.

```python
st.markdown("""<style>
/* Streamlit's main content area is its own scroll container, so a rule on html
   alone is likely a no-op. Cover html/body and the main pane. VERIFY THIS FIRST
   (check 1a) — this is the entire animation deliverable. */
html, body, [data-testid="stMain"], section.main { scroll-behavior: smooth; }

/* The id lives on the heading element itself, not on any wrapper, so target the
   heading. A data-testid selector here would very likely miss and every anchor
   jump would park the heading under the sticky header. */
:is(h1,h2,h3,h4,h5,h6)[id] { scroll-margin-top: 5rem; }

.rail a {
  display: block; padding: .35rem .5rem; margin: .1rem 0;
  border-left: 2px solid transparent;
  color: inherit; text-decoration: none; line-height: 1.3;
  transition: border-color .15s ease, background-color .15s ease,
              padding-left .15s ease;
}
.rail a:hover, .rail a:focus-visible {
  border-left-color: #B4232C;                  /* C_UNLABELED, the subject colour */
  background: rgba(180, 35, 44, .18);
  padding-left: .75rem;
}
/* currentColor rather than the red: on Streamlit's dark background #0E1117 a
   #B4232C ring is about 2.9:1, under WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11's 3:1 for focus
   indicators. currentColor inherits the theme's text colour and passes on both. */
.rail a:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid currentColor; outline-offset: 2px; }

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  html, body, [data-testid="stMain"], section.main { scroll-behavior: auto; }
  .rail a { transition: none; }
}
</style>""", unsafe_allow_html=True)
```

**The left border is the primary hover affordance, not the wash.** A translucent red is a
fixed value — it does not adapt to the theme, and 6% was invisible on dark. 18% reads on
both, but it is a compromise rather than a solution. Do **not** reach for
`@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` to fix it: Streamlit's theme comes from `config.toml`
or the user menu, not the OS, so a dark-Streamlit-on-light-OS visitor gets the wrong branch.
The border-left uses `C_UNLABELED`, the same red that marks the unlabeled bars in chart 1, so
the rail and the finding share one colour language.

**That block is the entire motion scope.**

**3. Sidebar layout with a deferred rail.** The rail must not render when the page is about
to `st.stop()` at line 95 (empty date range), or the links point at anchors never emitted.
(The line-39 `st.stop()` fires before the sidebar block at line 45, so that path never
renders a sidebar at all and needs no handling.) The rail also belongs *above* the filters
visually, so reserve the slot first and fill it after the guard:

```python
with st.sidebar:
    rail_slot = st.container()      # filled below, only if the page actually renders
    st.divider()
    st.header("Filters")
    picked = st.date_input(...)     # unchanged
    date_from, date_to = normalize_date_range(picked, lo, hi)
    st.caption(f"Source: {src.label}")
```

Then, after the `kpi.crashes == 0` guard at line 95:

```python
with rail_slot:
    render_rail()
```

**4. The appendix demotion.** Charts 4-9 (lines 175-255) move behind a demoted block.
**Settle check 1c before choosing the container.** Whatever is chosen, the move must carry
the `st.columns(2)` block at 186-208 and the `st.divider()` calls at 183 and 210 intact, and
each of the six keeps its `render_heading(key)` call so its anchor is still emitted.

The `4.`–`9.` prefixes stay in the headings. They are the reader's map back to the roadmap
and to `PROJECT_GUIDE.md`, and renumbering would break both.

**5. Tests.** `CLAUDE.md` requires a test for every new conditional. `tests/test_sections.py`
follows `tests/test_date_range.py`'s existing `sys.path.insert(..., "app")` pattern and
imports only `sections`, which pulls in nothing:


The last one is the important one: it mechanically prevents anyone later putting "30% of

### Honest note on "more interactive"

## Open Questions

1. **Three empirical checks that must run before the code is written.** None can be settled
   by argument, all are five minutes once a venv exists, and two of them can silently
   produce a feature that appears to work and does nothing.
   a. **Does `scroll-behavior` actually smooth the scroll?** Streamlit's main content area
      is its own scroll container, so a rule on `html` alone is likely a no-op — and this is
      the *entire* animation deliverable. Check first. If the listed selectors miss, find
      the real scrolling element in devtools and target it.
   b. **Do sidebar `<a href="#x">` links scroll the main pane at all?** It is the standard
      community TOC pattern and probably fine, but nothing here has been run. Also confirm
      the `class` attribute survives Streamlit's markdown sanitiser, and that re-clicking a
      link for the section already in `location.hash` is not a no-op.
   c. **Do anchors resolve inside a collapsed `st.expander`?** Almost certainly not —
      collapsed content is not laid out. **Fallback 1 (preferred):** give the appendix one
      anchored heading above the expander, point the whole Supporting-detail group at
      `#appendix`, and render the six names in the rail as plain text — an inventory rather
      than links, which is all Premise 2 asks of them. This keeps the demotion.
      **Fallback 2:** an always-rendered `st.container` under a demoted heading. Anchors
      work, but note this **forfeits the "charts 4-9 require a deliberate action to reach"
      criterion entirely** — an always-rendered container is reached by scrolling, which is
      today's behavior. Taking fallback 2 means the appendix demotion is deferred, not
      delivered; say so in the commit message rather than quietly failing the criterion.
      **Option 3:** `st.tabs` for the appendix — native, animated, genuinely in scope for
      this pass. Same caveat as the expander: tabs hide inactive content, so anchors into

2. **The rail is invisible on mobile.** Streamlit auto-collapses the sidebar below ~768px,
   so a phone visitor gets a hamburger and none of the hierarchy. For a link dropped into a

3. **Which chart did the observer stop at?** Unresolved, cheap to resolve, and it determines
   whether this feature fixes a defect or polishes a page that already worked. See The
   Assignment.
4. **Does the argument tier stay three items after the borough recovery lands?** The

5. **Should the appendix container be labelled honestly or neutrally?** "The nine standard

  misses Streamlit's real scroll container, and `[data-testid="stHeading"]

  `anchor=`, which all three approaches share, not by `SECTIONS`. The rationale is restated
